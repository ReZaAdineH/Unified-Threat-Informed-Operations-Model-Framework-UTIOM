# UTIOM for NIS2 and DORA

UTIOM is not a compliance framework and does not replace legal, regulatory or standards analysis. It can, however, help organisations turn governance and resilience requirements into operational evidence across Security Operations.

## NIS2

NIS2 requires in-scope organisations to implement appropriate cybersecurity risk-management measures and to assess whether those measures are effective.

UTIOM supports the operational side of that objective by linking:

**governance intent → relevant business consequence → threats and attack paths → engineered visibility → detection → response → validation → metrics and improvement**

This can help produce evidence that controls and operational capabilities are not merely documented, but used and tested.

Relevant UTIOM capabilities include:

- clear ownership and Security Operations strategy;
- Crown Jewel and critical-service prioritisation;
- threat-informed risk context;
- threat modelling and attack paths;
- logging and telemetry requirements;
- Detection Engineering and Detection QA;
- incident handling and escalation;
- response authority and exercises;
- effectiveness metrics and Continuous Improvement.

The canonical site specifically discusses UTIOM in relation to NIS2 Article 21 effectiveness assessment.

## DORA

DORA focuses on digital operational resilience for the financial sector. UTIOM can support its operational objectives through:

### ICT risk management

Crown Jewels, dependencies, Threat Profiling and attack paths provide a consequence-driven view of operational cyber risk.

### Detection and incident response

Threat Visibility and Threat Detection engineer the evidence and detection capability required to identify relevant adversary activity.

Response provides prepared decision paths, escalation, containment and recovery.

### Incident classification and escalation

Threat context, Crown Jewel impact and predefined decision gates can support consistent severity and escalation decisions.

### Testing and effectiveness

Detection QA, Purple Teaming, exercises and metrics create evidence about whether designed capabilities actually work.

### Learning and resilience

Continuous Improvement feeds incidents, tests and findings back into Strategy and engineering.

## Evidence over checkbox compliance

UTIOM's contribution is operational traceability. For example, an organisation should be able to show not only that it has a logging policy, but also:

- which critical services require which evidence;
- which relevant threat behaviours depend on that evidence;
- whether the telemetry is present and reliable;
- which detections use it;
- whether those detections have been tested;
- whether responders can act;
- what metrics show effectiveness.

That evidence can support governance, audit and regulatory conversations, while the applicable legal and compliance programme remains responsible for determining actual compliance.

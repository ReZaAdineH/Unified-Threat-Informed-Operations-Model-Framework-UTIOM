# UTIOM Overview

The Unified Threat-Informed Operations Model (UTIOM) is a security-operations operating model designed to connect strategy, business consequence, threat understanding, telemetry, detection, response and improvement into one coherent system.

UTIOM is not designed around a tool stack. It starts with the question of what the organisation must protect and why, then works outward through relevant threats, attack paths, required evidence and engineered capability.

## Core idea

The model follows this reasoning:

**Purpose first → strategy first → business consequence → relevant threats → attack paths → required evidence → engineered visibility → engineered detection → operational response → measurable improvement**

This creates a traceable relationship between executive intent and technical work.

A detection should not exist simply because a log source is available. It should exist because a relevant threat could affect something important, the attack path produces evidence, the evidence can be observed, the detection can be validated, and the response can reduce impact.

## Seven canonical phases

UTIOM has exactly seven lifecycle phases:

1. Vision
2. Strategy
3. Crown Jewels
4. Threat Visibility
5. Threat Detection
6. Response
7. Continuous Improvement

## Three operating pillars

The phases operate across three formal pillars:

- Leadership & Governance
- Engineering & Enablement
- Operations & Analysis

The pillars clarify ownership without turning the lifecycle into organisational silos.

## UTIOM as a living system

UTIOM treats Security Operations as a living product and operating system for the security programme.

That means the system must be:

- aligned to business consequence;
- informed by relevant threats;
- engineered rather than passively assembled;
- validated through evidence;
- measurable;
- continuously improved.

## What success looks like

A mature UTIOM implementation should be able to answer:

- Which business services matter most?
- Which threats are most relevant to them?
- Which attack paths matter?
- Which adversary behaviours must be observable?
- Is the required telemetry available and trustworthy?
- Which detections cover the important behaviours?
- Have those detections been tested?
- Can responders act inside the required time window?
- Are improvements measurable?
- Can capability claims be demonstrated with evidence?

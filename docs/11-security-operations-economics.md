# Security Operations Economics — Risk Reduction per Euro

UTIOM treats Security Operations investment as a prioritisation problem. Security resources are finite, so the objective is not to maximise tools, alerts, rules or telemetry volume. The objective is to increase **validated risk-reduction capability per unit of investment**.

## Why Security Operations becomes expensive

Security programmes often accumulate cost through disconnected decisions:

- more telemetry without a clear evidence requirement;
- more detection content without threat relevance;
- overlapping tools bought by different teams;
- analyst time consumed by low-value alerts;
- response processes that depend on manual coordination;
- controls applied uniformly rather than according to consequence and threat;
- maturity activity that cannot be linked to measurable outcomes.

This creates operational cost without proving equivalent risk reduction.

## The UTIOM investment chain

UTIOM makes investment traceable:

**Business consequence**  
→ **relevant threats**  
→ **attack paths**  
→ **required evidence**  
→ **telemetry requirements**  
→ **detection requirements**  
→ **response requirements**  
→ **validated capability**  
→ **measured outcome**

Every major engineering or technology investment should be able to explain where it sits on this chain.

## High-fidelity over indiscriminate coverage

UTIOM does not argue for fewer detections merely to reduce cost. It argues for **purposeful detection**: detections engineered around relevant adversary behaviour, critical services and realistic attack paths.

This can reduce waste by prioritising:

- telemetry that is needed for important behaviours;
- high-fidelity detection logic;
- validation of the detections that matter most;
- response preparation for high-consequence scenarios;
- engineering work that closes demonstrated gaps.

## Cost of bad visibility

Telemetry has acquisition, storage, processing, engineering and operational costs. Collecting everything indefinitely is not the same as being visible.

Threat Visibility asks which evidence is required and whether it can be trusted. This makes telemetry architecture an investment decision rather than a default ingestion exercise.

## Cost of response delay

The Response Horizon adds another economic dimension. When decision authority, evidence and playbooks are absent, organisations pay for delay through larger blast radius, longer outages, more investigation work and fewer containment options.

Preparing authority and response options before the incident can reduce the cost of intervention when an incident occurs.

## What should be measured

A useful economic view combines financial inputs with operational outcomes such as:

- Crown Jewel coverage;
- relevant-threat coverage;
- validated detection rate;
- telemetry quality and assurance;
- false-positive operational cost;
- analyst effort;
- decision latency;
- MTTD and MTTC;
- containment effectiveness;
- recurrence and improvement closure.

The purpose is to ask whether additional investment produced additional validated capability.

## Security Operations as a product

Treating Security Operations as a product supports better investment decisions. Product management asks what users and the business need, what features create value, which defects matter, what should be prioritised, and whether the delivered capability is actually used and effective.

UTIOM applies the same discipline to Security Operations.

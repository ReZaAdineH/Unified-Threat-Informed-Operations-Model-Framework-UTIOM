# UTIOM Philosophy and Seven Laws

UTIOM was created to address a recurring problem in security operations: organisations often invest in technology, telemetry and controls without a sufficiently strong chain of reasoning connecting those investments to business consequence and relevant adversary behaviour.

The framework therefore begins with purpose and consequence, not tooling.

## Philosophy

UTIOM is built around the following principles:

- strategy before sensors;
- business consequence before indiscriminate control coverage;
- crown jewels drive prioritisation;
- relevant threats shape architecture;
- threat profiling identifies what matters most;
- threat modelling and attack paths explain how compromise can occur;
- visibility is deliberately engineered rather than passively accumulated;
- detection is engineered from threat relevance, protected assets, attack paths and available evidence;
- missing telemetry becomes a Telemetry Engineering requirement;
- detections must be validated rather than merely deployed;
- response effectiveness depends on prepared authority, evidence and decision capability;
- Purple Teaming, Detection QA and adversary emulation validate the system;
- improvement must feed back into strategy and engineering;
- security operations are a living system and a living product;
- capability should be demonstrated through evidence, not merely claimed;
- technology supports the operating model; it does not define it.

## Seven Laws

### 1. Business survival defines security

Security capability must trace back to the business consequences it is intended to protect. A control or detection that cannot be related to a meaningful business outcome should be challenged.

### 2. Strategy before sensors

Telemetry and technology should follow a security operations strategy. Available data should not determine the strategy simply because it is convenient to collect.

### 3. Crown jewels drive prioritisation

Security resources are finite. UTIOM concentrates stronger defensive capability around critical services, crown jewels and the dependencies whose compromise could create material consequences.

### 4. Threats shape architecture

Relevant adversary behaviour, threat models and attack paths should shape visibility, detection and response design. UTIOM does not treat all possible threats as equally important.

### 5. Visibility is a design decision

Missing evidence is an engineering issue. Where an important adversary behaviour cannot be observed, the organisation should create, prioritise and own a Telemetry Engineering requirement or explicitly accept the resulting risk.

### 6. Operations is continuous response

Threat intelligence, threat hunting, detection engineering, monitoring, analysis and containment are not disconnected functions. They are different expressions of incident response at different distances from impact.

### 7. Improvement is mandatory

Incidents, exercises, adversary emulation, Detection QA and operational metrics should create measurable improvements. A lesson that does not change the system is not yet an improvement.

## The role of technology

UTIOM is technology-agnostic. SIEM, EDR, XDR, SOAR, cloud security services, threat-intelligence platforms and automation can all support the model, but none of them define it.

The framework asks first what capability is required, then which technology can implement that capability effectively.

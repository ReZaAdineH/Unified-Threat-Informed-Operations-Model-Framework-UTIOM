# SOC Operating Model — The UTIOM View

A Security Operations Center (SOC) is not defined by a room, SIEM or monitoring team. In UTIOM, the SOC is one organisational expression of a broader **Security Operations capability** whose purpose is to reduce and control cyber risk through preparation, visibility, detection, analysis, response and continuous learning.

## What is a SOC operating model?

A SOC operating model defines how purpose, ownership, people, process, engineering, technology, decision authority, measurement and improvement work together.

UTIOM treats that operating model as one connected lifecycle rather than separate management, engineering and operational silos.

## Seven operating components

The canonical UTIOM lifecycle provides seven connected components:

1. **Vision** — why Security Operations exists and how success is defined.
2. **Strategy** — threat profile, priorities and operating direction.
3. **Crown Jewels** — critical services, dependencies, trust boundaries and attack paths.
4. **Threat Visibility** — telemetry architecture and evidence assurance.
5. **Threat Detection** — threat-informed detection engineering.
6. **Response** — analysis, decision, escalation, containment and recovery.
7. **Continuous Improvement** — measurement, validation, learning and backlog management.

## Internal, outsourced and hybrid SOCs

UTIOM can be used with three broad delivery structures:

- **Internal SOC** — capability and ownership primarily reside inside the organisation.
- **Outsourced / managed SOC** — monitoring or response services are delivered by an MSSP/MDR or other provider.
- **Hybrid SOC** — capability and ownership are distributed between internal teams and external providers.

The delivery model can change; the traceability requirement does not.

An outsourced provider may operate the SIEM or perform triage, but the organisation still needs clarity on:

- business purpose;
- Crown Jewels;
- threat priorities;
- required telemetry;
- detection requirements;
- decision authority;
- containment responsibilities;
- evidence and metrics;
- continuous improvement ownership.

## Security Operations as a product

UTIOM treats Security Operations as a living product. Like a product, it needs:

- a clear vision;
- ownership;
- an evolving strategy;
- engineered capabilities;
- measurable outcomes;
- user and operational feedback;
- an improvement backlog.

The objective is not to operate more tools. It is to deliver increasingly reliable risk-reduction capability.

## Security Operations and Incident Response

UTIOM treats Incident Response as the operating mode of Security Operations rather than a disconnected late-stage function.

Threat intelligence, threat modelling, detection engineering, threat hunting, monitoring, analysis, containment, forensics and lessons learned are different expressions of the same response mission at different distances from business impact.

## Building a SOC from scratch

A greenfield SOC should not start with a SIEM procurement. UTIOM begins with:

**Vision → Strategy → Crown Jewels and relevant threats → attack paths → required evidence → telemetry architecture → detection engineering → response → measurement**

Technology choices then become consequences of requirements rather than substitutes for requirements.

## Improving a running SOC

An existing SOC can begin with the UTIOM assessment tools, identify maturity and capability gaps, create a roadmap, engineer the priority changes, validate them, measure the results and reassess.
